Etalon transmission and reflection

Set the mirror reflectance and free spectral range of an etalon and the length of the input pulse. The page draws the partial beams, the transmitted and reflected spectra, and the pulses in time, and lists the finesse and bandwidths. To go the other way, enter a bandwidth and a spacing under "Etalon for a given bandwidth" and the page gives the mirror reflectance. "Tuning by tilt" shows how far a tilt moves the peaks. Everything recalculates as you drag. Equations are at the bottom.

Examples:

Two equal, lossless mirrors; R is the same at every wavelength. The input pulse is a Gaussian, given by its duration, by its bandwidth, or by both. With both, the extra bandwidth is put in as a linear chirp: the spectra depend only on the bandwidth, the pulses in time on both. The offset moves the comb of peaks under the pulse: in the lab that is a gap change of less than λ/2. The tilt is the angle of incidence outside the etalon; it moves the peaks to higher wavenumber (see "Tuning by tilt"). The FSR entered here is the one at normal incidence. λ₀ and n set the gap, the nm values and the size of the tilt shift.

Each number is that partial beam's share of the incident intensity; line weight follows its field amplitude. Beams below 0.1 % of the first one are left out. The beams are drawn tilted so the bounces can be seen; at normal incidence they lie on top of each other and interfere.

Etalon for a given bandwidth

Enter the bandwidth you want for one transmission peak, as a FWHM or as the decay time of the transmitted pulse, and the spacing, as a free spectral range, a gap thickness or a multiple of the input bandwidth. The table gives the mirror reflectance, the tilt needed to tune over one FSR and the other parameters.

Changing a unit converts the number. Values in nm and the tilt use λ₀, the gap thickness uses n, and "× input bandwidth" uses the input pulse, all from the inputs above. The button copies R and the FSR into those inputs so the plots show this etalon.

For a smooth, continuous decay in time, use a spacing of about 2 × the input bandwidth. The neighbouring peaks are then too weak for the round trips to show as ripple on the tail. At 1.5 × the spectrum is already a single peak, but the tail still carries about ±18 % ripple. The table lists both numbers.

Tuning by tilt

A fixed air-gap etalon is already tunable. Tilting it moves every peak to higher wavenumber, and a tilt of θ ≈ √(2·FSR/ν) covers one FSR: 7.6° for 172 cm⁻¹ and 11.6° for 394 cm⁻¹ at 515 nm. Tilt plus hopping between orders gives continuous coverage, and with a thin gap the walk-off is negligible.

peak shift, in FSR whole numbers of FSR tilt set above

The shift grows as θ². Where the curve crosses a dashed line the comb is back where it started, one order further on, so any wavelength can be reached with a tilt below the first crossing.

Tilt only moves peaks to higher wavenumber (shorter wavelength). Keep a degree or two of tilt even when none is needed for tuning, so the reflected light does not go back into the laser. Not included: the change of R with angle and polarisation, and the broadening from beam divergence, δν ≈ ν · tan θ · δθ.

Transmission

Narrow peaks, one per FSR, each reaching 1. Between the peaks the etalon still passes 1/(1 + F); that floor only matters where the pulse has light.

Reflection

etalon reflection input spectrum reflected spectrum FWHM of an output band

The complement, 1 − T: zeros at the transmission peaks and bright bands between them. A bright band is never narrower than FSR/2, and it gets wider as R goes up. Set the offset to ±0.5 to put a band on the pulse centre.

Drag across either plot to zoom both in x, double-click to zoom out, hover to read values. The input spectrum is scaled to a peak of 1.

Numbers

Pulses in time

Transmitted

Copies of the input one round trip apart, each weaker by R². When the round trip is shorter than the pulse they merge into a sharp rise and an exponential tail.

Reflected

The first copy comes straight off the front mirror with R of the input. The later ones leak back out of the gap with the opposite sign; at low R only the first two matter.

Intensity in units of the input peak intensity; the input pulse is centred at t = 0. A pulse that is not transform-limited is given a linear chirp. Drag to zoom both, double-click to zoom out.

Equations

  1. Round-trip phase and free spectral range

    δ = 4π n d cos θ · ν = 2π ν / FSR,  FSR = 1 / (2 n d cos θ),  trt = 1 / (c · FSR)

    ν is the wavenumber in cm⁻¹, d the gap, θ the angle inside the gap (0 here for the listed gap). A transmission peak sits wherever δ is a multiple of 2π.

  2. Transmission (Airy function)

    T = 1 / [1 + F sin²(δ/2)],  F = 4R / (1 − R)²

    Maximum 1, minimum 1/(1 + F) = [(1 − R)/(1 + R)]².

  3. Reflection

    Re = 1 − T = F sin²(δ/2) / [1 + F sin²(δ/2)]

    Zero at every transmission peak for any R, because the two mirrors are equal and lossless. Maximum F/(1 + F).

  4. Widths and finesse

    transmission peak FWHM = (2 FSR / π) arcsin(1/√F) ≈ FSR / ℱ,  ℱ = π√F / 2 = π√R / (1 − R)
    reflection bright band FWHM = FSR [1 − (2/π) arcsin(1/√(2 + F))] ≥ FSR / 2

    The transmission peak has a half-maximum only when F > 1, that is R > 3 − 2√2 = 17.2 %. Below that the transmission never drops to one half and the table shows a dash.

  5. Mirror reflectance for a given peak width

    F = 1 / sin²(π · FWHM / (2 FSR)),  R = [F + 2 − 2√(F + 1)] / F

    Equation 4 solved for F, then F = 4R/(1 − R)² solved for R. It needs FWHM < FSR. A gap thickness is turned into a spacing with FSR = 1/(2nd); 1 cm⁻¹ = 0.029979 THz = λ₀²/10⁷ nm (λ₀ in nm).

    from a decay time τc:  R = exp[−trt / (2τc)],  trt = 1 / (c · FSR)
    neighbouring peaks: S(FSR) = exp[−4 ln 2 (FSR/Δν)²];  ripple on the tail ≈ ±4 √S(FSR)

    τc is the 1/e time of the transmitted intensity. The ripple is the beat between the main peak and its two neighbours, for a transform-limited input with a peak on the pulse centre: ±18 % at FSR = 1.5 Δν, ±1.6 % at 2 Δν.

  6. Tuning by tilt

    νm(θ) = νm(0) / cos θin,  sin θ = n sin θin,  shift ≈ ν θin² / 2
    one FSR at cos θin = 1 − FSR/ν, so θin ≈ √(2 FSR/ν);  walk-off per round trip = 2d tan θin

    θ is the angle of incidence outside the etalon and θin the angle in the gap (equal for an air gap). From equation 1, a tilt replaces FSR by FSR/cos θin and adds ν0(1 − cos θin)/FSR to the peak offset; the plots and tables use those values.

  7. Input pulse

    S(ν) = exp[−4 ln 2 (ν − ν0)² / Δν²],  a(t) = exp[−2 ln 2 (1 + iC) t² / τ²],  c τ Δν = 0.4413 √(1 + C²)

    Gaussian, τ and Δν both intensity FWHM. Transform-limited (C = 0): 200 fs gives 73.6 cm⁻¹, 70 fs gives 210 cm⁻¹. When both τ and Δν are entered, the linear chirp C is whatever makes them consistent; Δν cannot be smaller than the transform limit of τ.

  8. Pulse energy transmitted and reflected

    ET / Ein = [(1 − R)/(1 + R)] · [1 + 2 Σm≥1 Rm exp(−2π² m² σ² / FSR²) cos(2π m x0)],  ER = Ein − ET

    σ = Δν / 2.355 and x0 is the peak offset in units of FSR. This is the Airy function written as a Fourier series and averaged over the Gaussian spectrum.

  9. Pulses in time

    ET(t) = (1 − R) Σm≥0 Rm eimφ a(t − m trt)
    ER(t) = √R [a(t) − (1 − R) Σm≥1 Rm−1 eimφ a(t − m trt)]

    a(t) is the input field envelope and φ = −2πx0 the round-trip phase at the pulse centre. The intensity of the transmitted train falls by R² per round trip: 1/e time trt / (2 ln(1/R)), which is 1/(2πc · FWHM) at high finesse.

Not included: mirror loss, unequal mirrors, the wavelength dependence and phase of the coatings, dispersion in the gap, beam walk-off and divergence, surface flatness. All of these lower the real finesse and peak transmission.
